Everything sits at its real position and real size: the planets where they are today, 1,982 naked-eye stars at their measured distances, the Milky Way, its neighbours and the cosmic web out to the edge of the observable universe. Brightness is compressed so faint things stay visible, and fast processes (orbits, explosions, mergers) are sped up. The few objects too small to see next to their surroundings (a quasar's engine, a kilonova) are drawn magnified, and the scale bar shows their true size. The Halo, the ring ship that wanders between wonders, is the one invented thing here. Simulations: Schwarzschild light bending, relativistic Doppler beaming, Keplerian and flat rotation curves, restricted N-body galaxy collision, Roche-lobe mass transfer, light-echo geometry, gravitational-wave inspiral.
